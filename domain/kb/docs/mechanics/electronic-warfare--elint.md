# Electronic Warfare › ELINT

Source: Official wiki (wiki.hoodedhorse.com), game 0.6.2.6:260903-1430

## Body
ELINT detectors (such as the Pinard) allows a ship to passively detect enemy radar emissions, such as those from search radars or fire control radars, at up to 1.25x of that radar's max range. One ELINT module will show a rough line of bearing toward the emitter; a crossfix requires bearing-only tracks from ELINT on two or more different friendly ships on the team network (two modules on the same ship do not crossfix each other). ELINT will also be able to identify what type of radar it is (or more specifically, it can reveal the ELINT category of the radar) and will display it on the ELINT LOB or crossfix. If the ship is equipped with multiple types of radars, the track will display all detected types.

The categories are generally self explanatory, most internal search radars (spyglass, huntress, etc) are Search/Track. All turreted fire control radars (Bullseye, Pinpoint) are Fire Control. The Early Warning Radar is the only radar that will be specified as Early Warning. Parallax using the locking function will still classify as Search/Track. Bloodhound is classified as Long Range Tracking, the only radar with that category.

Most directional radars like FCRs also have sidelobe patterns, allowing ELINT to detect these radars at reduced range even if they're not pointed at the ELINT set. These sidelobe patterns can be viewed on their component page here: https://wiki.hoodedhorse.com/NEBULOUS_Fleet_Command/Components

ELINT is not able to detect jamming.

ELINT is not able to detect integrated FCRs on PD turrets

ELINT is not able to detect illuminators.

ELINT is not masked by hull, and a ship with an ELINT detector on either side will not have a blindspot between the ELINT sets (as ELINT is measured from the center of the ship rather than from the set itself).
